# Dragging through the text selects what it crosses, which typing then
# replaces. The press has to land inside the text: a line is only as wide as
# what is written on it, and a press beside it belongs to no line at all.
# The drag runs from where "goes" starts, found by looking rather than by a
# pixel count, to past the end of the line, so what it selects is everything
# from there on: "text goes here" becomes "text X". The end of the drag sits
# far right of the text so it is past the line's end whatever width a
# platform's font gives it. A word rather than part of one: text recognition
# places words, and a mark inside one snaps to its start. Not the last word on
# the line either: the caret stands against it, and a reader takes the two
# together and makes a different letter of it.
